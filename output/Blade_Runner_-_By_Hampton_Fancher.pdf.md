# Blade Runner Script Report

## About the Script

**Title:** Blade Runner  
**Author:** Hampton Fancher, David Peoples (screenplay), Philip K. Dick (novel)  
**Genre:** Science Fiction, Neo-noir  
**Date:** 1982  

**Plot Summary:**  
In a dystopian future, ex-cop Rick Deckard is tasked with hunting down and 'retiring' a group of rogue replicants — bioengineered beings indistinguishable from humans. While tracking down the replicants, he becomes embroiled in their struggle for freedom and identity, leading to reflections on what it means to be human.

## Locations

- **Tyrell Corporation:** Includes the locker room, air-filtered corridors, inner sanctum, mansion, preserve, dining room, and den, portraying the corporate might and wealth.
- **Deckard's Environments:** Consist of his apartment and car, emphasizing his solitary and introspective nature.
- **Sebastian's World:** His apartment and ambulance are depicted, showing a mix of eccentricity and isolation.
- **City Streets and Districts:** Urban locations such as various city streets, alleys, Chinatown, and the area around the old Opera House provide a gritty, bustling backdrop.
- **Taffey's Bar:** A typical cyberpunk nightlife spot, adding to the film's noir feel.
- **Gym and Indoor Settings:** Scenes include the gym in the basement of Sebastian’s building, Leon’s hotel lobby and room, Hannibal Chew’s shop, and the dressing room backstage at the Opera House, diversifying the urban landscape.
- **Outdoor Scenes:** Feature deserts, woods, lakes, highways, and aerial city views from vehicles like the spinner, highlighting contrasts between the natural and artificial.

## Characters

- **Rick Deckard:** A former police officer known as a "Blade Runner" whose task is to search out and retire replicants—bioengineered beings who look exactly like humans.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Jake Gyllenhaal, Michael Fassbender, Oscar Isaac

- **Roy Batty:** A highly advanced replicant with superhuman abilities and leader of the group of fugitive Nexus-6 replicants seeking to extend their lifespans.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Tom Hardy, Idris Elba, Alexander Skarsgård

- **Rachael:** A sophisticated replicant who was created with implanted memories, making her unaware that she isn't truly human.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Alicia Vikander, Margot Robbie, Ana de Armas

- **Gaff:** A cityspeak-speaking, cane-wielding fellow Blade Runner who follows Deckard throughout his investigation and has a knack for crafting origami figures.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Benicio Del Toro, Javier Bardem, Gael García Bernal

- **Pris Stratton:** A pleasure model replicant who uses her charm and wit to survive. She partners with Roy Batty in their quest for more life.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Zendaya, Emma Stone, Saoirse Ronan

- **Eldon Tyrell:** The wealthy and visionary founder of the Tyrell Corporation who specializes in creating replicants with highly advanced intelligence.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Anthony Hopkins, Jeremy Irons, Mark Rylance

- **J.F. Sebastian:** A lonely genetic designer and toy maker with a degenerative disease; he befriends the rogue replicants seeking answers about their existence.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Paul Dano, Rami Malek, Eddie Redmayne

- **Leon Kowalski:** A strong yet dim-witted replicant tasked with the physical labor in off-world colonies. Known for his emotional instability when confronted.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Dave Bautista, John Cena, Jason Momoa

- **Zhora Salome:** A replicant trained as an exotic dancer with a dangerous edge who tries to evade Deckard when he begins his hunt.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Tessa Thompson, Sofia Boutella, Eiza González

- **Bryant:** The gruff, no-nonsense police chief who pushes Deckard to come out of retirement to hunt down the loose replicants.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Jeffrey Dean Morgan, Bryan Cranston, J.K. Simmons

- **Holden:** A senior Blade Runner who is sidelined early in the story after being injured by Leon during a Voight-Kampff test designed to identify replicants.
  - **Suggested Actors:** Jon Hamm, Riz Ahmed, David Harbour

## Props

- **Voight-Kampff machine:** A polygraph-like machine used to measure bodily functions such as respiration, blush response, heart rate, and eye movement in response to emotionally provocative questions. It's used primarily to identify replicants.
- **Spinner:** A type of flying car used by the police and some other entities in Los Angeles, 2019. Spinners are capable of both vertical and propelled flight, as well as ground transportation.
- **Artificial Owl:** A synthetic owl owned by the Tyrell Corporation. It represents the theme of artificial life and the blurred lines between real and synthetic to viewers.
- **Blaster:** A handgun used by the main character, Rick Deckard, to 'retire' replicants by force. It's a key prop symbolizing his role as a Blade Runner.
- **Photographs:** Images that replicants keep at hand as part of implanted memories or emotional anchors. They serve as a symbolic attachment to their false pasts.
- **Esper Machine:** A sophisticated computer device capable of scanning and 'enhancing' photographs to provide detailed visual information from a picture, crucial for detective work.
- **Umbrellas with lighted handles:** Common among the citizens of futuristic Los Angeles, used to navigate the perpetually wet environment. The illuminated handles add to the neon, dystopian atmosphere.
- **Origami Figures:** Small paper sculptures made by Gaff, Deckard's rival. These figures serve as symbolic messages or warnings throughout the film.

## Author

### Accomplishments

- **Hampton Fancher:**
  - Screenwriting: Noted for his work as a screenwriter and film producer. One of his primary achievements is co-writing the screenplay for 'Blade Runner.'

- **David Peoples:**
  - Screenwriting: Renowned for his screenwriting and contributions to other major films beyond 'Blade Runner.'

- **Philip K. Dick:**
  - Literary Work: A prolific novelist and short story writer, celebrated for his speculative fiction exploring complex themes such as altered realities, identity, and the nature of humanity. His novel 'Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?' served as the basis for 'Blade Runner.'

### Other Movie Scripts

- **Hampton Fancher:** The Minus Man (1999), a psychological thriller directed by him.
- **David Peoples:** 
  - Unforgiven (1992), a critically acclaimed Western that won multiple Oscars.
  - 12 Monkeys (1995), a science fiction film exploring time travel.

### Worked With

- **Hampton Fancher:** Worked closely with filmmaker Ridley Scott during the production of 'Blade Runner.'
- **David Peoples:** Collaborated with Clint Eastwood for 'Unforgiven' and worked with Terry Gilliam for '12 Monkeys.'
- **Philip K. Dick:** Many of Dick's works have been adapted into films and television series, with notable adaptations including 'Total Recall,' 'Minority Report,' and 'The Man in the High Castle.'
