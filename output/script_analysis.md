
# Minority Report Script Markdown Report

## About the Script
- **Title**: Minority Report
- **Author**: Scott Frank
- **Genre**: Science Fiction, Thriller
- **Date**: 2002
- **Plot Summary**: The movie script "Minority Report," written by Scott Frank, explores significant themes such as free will versus determinism. Set in a dystopian future, the plot revolves around a specialized police force called Precrime, which uses a trio of precognitive individuals to prevent murders before they occur. The story follows former officer John Anderson, who is wrongfully accused of a future murder he has yet to commit. As he struggles to prove his innocence, Anderson exposes a conspiracy that questions the ethical underpinnings of the Precrime system, challenging concepts of fate, justice, and human agency.

## Locations
1. **Washington D.C.**: Set in a futuristic version of the city, where high-tech advancements are rampant, and the cityscape features a mix of old and new architecture. It's the core setting for the crime department and political intrigue.
   
2. **Precrime Headquarters**: A highly secure, technologically advanced building that serves as the center for the Precrime unit. Featured with state-of-the-art equipment and systems for crime prediction using psychics known as 'Precogs.'

3. **The Temple**: Within Precrime Headquarters, this is a sanctum where the Precogs are kept. A large, serene chamber that contrasts with the sterile enforcement areas, it is designed for their comfort and maximum efficiency.

4. **Anderton's Apartment**: Personal residence of the protagonist, Detective John Anderton. Portrays a blend of advanced tech and personal memorabilia, reflecting his troubled past and dedication to the Precrime mission.

5. **Crowded Public Streets**: Vibrant urban environments bustling with people, showcasing the integration of digital advertisements, surveillance, and transport systems, highlighting the mix of high-tech and human elements.

6. **Fiddler's Green**: A lush, park-like virtual environment where citizens can escape reality. Symbolizes people's retreats in a heavily policed world and conveys themes of deep-seated unrest and longing for freedom.

## Characters
- **John Anderton**: The protagonist and chief of the Pre-Crime unit, John is determined yet vulnerable. Haunted by the disappearance of his son, he fiercely believes in the Pre-Crime program until he finds himself accused of a future crime. His journey becomes one of self-discovery and redemption.
  - **Suggested Actors**: Chris Evans, Michael B. Jordan, Jake Gyllenhaal

- **Danny Witwer**: A sharp and ambitious federal agent brought in to audit the Pre-Crime program. Witwer is skeptical of the system's infallibility and serves as an antagonist to Anderton, adding tension and raising ethical questions about justice and free will.
  - **Suggested Actors**: John David Washington, Daniel Kaluuya, Rami Malek

- **Agatha**: One of the three precogs, Agatha is the most gifted and essential to the Pre-Crime process. Her character is portrayed with vulnerability and innocence yet holds immense power and knowledge, making her pivotal to revealing the truth about the system.
  - **Suggested Actors**: Anya Taylor-Joy, Saoirse Ronan, Florence Pugh

- **Lamar Burgess**: The seemingly benevolent director of Pre-Crime, Lamar is a key figure in the program's success. However, his motivations become questionable as layers of deceit and self-preservation are unveiled, revealing a complex villain.
  - **Suggested Actors**: Bryan Cranston, Gary Oldman, Mark Strong

- **Lara Clarke**: Anderton's estranged wife, Lara is deeply connected to his past trauma. Her character facilitates emotional vulnerability and offers moments of comfort and revelation, playing a crucial role in Anderton's quest for truth.
  - **Suggested Actors**: Rachel McAdams, Jessica Chastain, Emily Blunt

- **Wally the Caretaker**: A minor but memorable character who looks after the needs of the precogs. Often seen as quirky, Wally provides comic relief while representing the moral ambiguity and blind trust given to the Pre-Crime system.
  - **Suggested Actors**: Taika Waititi, Simon Pegg, Jim Parsons

- **Gordon Fletcher**: A member of the Pre-Crime team, Fletcher is professional yet staunchly loyal to the system. His belief in Pre-Crime's mission serves as a foil to Anderton's growing doubts and ethical dilemmas.
  - **Suggested Actors**: Anthony Mackie, Lakeith Stanfield, Sebastian Stan

## Props
- **Precog Discs**: Advanced technology used for surveillance and crime prevention, central to the story’s dystopian setting.
- **Holographic Scanner**: Emphasizes the futuristic technological environment.
- **IdentiScan Device**: Part of the advanced surveillance technology in the narrative.
- **Police Hovercraft**: A symbol of the high-tech society and its control mechanisms.
- **TV Monitor**: Represents constant surveillance and information dissemination.
- **Scissors**: Symbolizes the predicted violent act against Carol Palmer, essential to the plot.
- **Sewing Basket**: Contrasts domestic normalcy with underlying tension, expanding character relationships.
- **Barbecue Grill**: Highlights domestic life, contrasting with themes of tension and violence.
- **Neurotissue Samples**: Represents scientific exploration of precognitive abilities, important for the story's premise.
- **Gun**: Illustrates the threat of violence, driving conflict within the narrative.

  - **Emmy Awards**: He won an Emmy Award for Outstanding Directing for a Limited Series for his work on 'The Queen’s Gambit,' which he also co-created and wrote.
  - **Notable Films**: Frank has been involved in numerous successful films, gaining respect in Hollywood for his skill in adapting complex stories into compelling screenplays.

- **Other Movie Scripts**:
  - **Get Shorty (1995)**: Adapted from Elmore Leonard’s novel, this film was a critical and commercial success, earning Frank an Academy Award nomination.
