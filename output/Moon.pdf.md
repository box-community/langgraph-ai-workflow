# Moon Script Report

## About the Script

**Title:** Moon  
**Author:** Nathan Parker  
**Genre:** Science Fiction  
**Date:** 2009  

The script "Moon" by Nathan Parker, based on a story by Duncan Jones, is a riveting exploration of identity, reality, and ethics centered on the character Sam Bell. Set on the Moon, where Sam is conducting a solitary three-year stint for Lunar Industries, the narrative introduces an unexpected twist with the appearance of another version of Sam—his clone. This discovery propels the script into a deep exploration of profound questions surrounding the nature of identity and the moral implications of cloning.

Key elements of the script include:

1. **Duality of Identity:** The presence of two Sams challenges the conventional understanding of self. This duality allows the script to delve into discussions about what constitutes an individual’s identity and whether a clone possesses the same rights and consciousness as the original.

2. **Themes of Isolation and Loneliness:** The lunar setting accentuates Sam's isolation, which is a significant theme throughout the script. The stark, barren lunar landscape mirrors his emotional state, and the emergence of a clone adds complexity to his solitude.

3. **Morality and Ethics:** The discovery of a hidden chamber with unawakened clones raises ethical questions about the company’s practices and the treatment of clones as mere tools for labor. The script challenges the audience to consider the morality of cloning and its implications on human rights.

4. **Recurring Dreams and Hallucinations:** Sam’s visions of a girl in a yellow dress serve as a motif reflecting his mental decay. These hallucinations add a layer of psychological depth to the character, underscoring his struggle with reality and sanity.

5. **Conflict and Survival:** As Sam 1 and Sam 2 uncover the truth about their existence, they devise escape plans to survive and expose the company’s schemes. This narrative thread introduces tension and urgency, especially with an impending "Rescue Unit" that threatens their autonomy and freedom.

6. **Legacy and Freedom:** Ultimately, the script forces its characters to reconcile with their engineered existence. The climax involves making pivotal choices that impact their future and legacy, questioning what freedom truly means for a clone.

"Moon" intricately weaves these thematic elements against the backdrop of lunar desolation, crafting a compelling story that engages with significant philosophical questions about human existence and rights in the age of technological advancement.

## Locations

1. **Sarang Lunar Base:** The primary setting of the movie, this is where Sam Bell is stationed on the Moon for his three-year contract. It is depicted as an isolated and sterile environment, emphasizing Sam's loneliness and disconnection from Earth.

2. **Lunar Landscape:** The vast, empty, and desolate surface of the Moon. The lunar landscape forms a stark backdrop to Sam's experiences and mental deterioration, highlighting themes of isolation.

3. **Helium3 Harvesting Facility:** Part of the Sarang Lunar Base, where Sam works to extract Helium3, a key energy source for Earth. This location underscores the reason behind Sam's mission and the corporate exploitation involved.

4. **Hidden Clone Chamber:** A secret chamber within the base that holds numerous unawakened clones of Sam Bell. This location reveals the true nature of Sam's existence and adds a layer of ethical dilemmas to the narrative.

5. **Earth Communication Room:** A location within the base where Sam attempts to communicate with Earth. This room symbolizes his longing for connection and realization of manipulation by the company.
